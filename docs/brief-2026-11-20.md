## 1. The headline: three new internet-facing RCEs were disclosed *and* exploited inside one week

**VMware vCenter CVE-2026-123890 (CVSS 9.8)** is the most consequential item on the board. A China-linked espionage group — Armory Castle, formerly UNC5221 / "Warp Panda" — exploited it for **more than two months** before Broadcom shipped a patch on 19 November. Google's Mandiant documented the chain: initial access through a **BeyondTrust Remote Support zero-day (CVE-2026-102764)**, pivot to an internal Linux appliance, then a purpose-built tool, **Bonesaw**, to obtain an authenticated `administrator@vsphere.local` session. From there the attackers stole the LMDB database, extracted **SAML signing material**, minted an SSO administrator account, **forged SAML tokens**, took the vCenter master encryption key and database credentials, and moved on to an ESXi host. A **SLIMSAVAGE web shell** provided persistence. Broadcom's advisory does not mention in-the-wild exploitation; Mandiant's does.

This is not "a CVE you should patch". It is a complete **identity-compromise chain against the hypervisor layer** that runs most enterprise workloads, and it demonstrates that patching alone would not have helped — the SAML and encryption-key theft is the damage.

The same week delivered four more critical remote code execution flaws in widely deployed software:

| Product | ID | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **VMware vCenter** | CVE-2026-123890 | Auth bypass → SSO admin, SAML forgery. Exploited since Sept. No fix for 7.0. |
| **NGINX ("NGINX2Root")** | CVE-2026-123007, CVE-2026-123633 (+3) | HTTP/2 and HTTP/3 memory corruption, **public PoC**; Xint chained a Linux SCTP kernel bug to escape a container and get **root on the host**. |
| **OpenJDK ("Java4Shell")** | CVE-2026-114938 | Unauthenticated RCE against default Jira, Confluence, Jenkins, Keycloak. Already being scanned at internet scale. |
| **Apache Axis** | CVE-2026-90436 | CVSS 9.8, exploited against **Confluence Data Center** and N-able N-central. CISA KEV, remediation due 21 Nov. |
| **Apache Roller** | CVE-2026-129448 | Unsafe XML-RPC deserialization, unauthenticated RCE, **confirmed actively exploited** by Apache. |
| **PHP ("PHP2Shell")** | CVE-2026-109618 / -109619 | Confirmed exploited; a third flaw (CVE-2026-115560) is being patched now. ~70% of the web runs PHP. |
| **Cisco Expressway** | CVE-2026-76466 | CVSS 9.8 unauth root RCE; **exploited days after a public PoC**, against a Russian organisation. |

The Apache Axis intrusion is worth reading closely because it shows how fast a service account becomes a business-critical breach: Rapid7 observed **a service account credential held within four hours** of the first exploit request, a **production cloud data warehouse being queried**, and a **Microsoft 365 sign-in using an integration service account roughly twelve hours in**.

## 2. Ransomware has shifted from encryption to pure data extortion — and healthcare is the target

**8-Digits Team** is running an extortion campaign against US hospitals and schools with demands **exceeding $500M**, threatening to publish patient and student records across at least six states. One alleged member was **extradited from Italy**. The encryption is pressure; the patient records are the product; and the victim's regulatory disclosure obligation is part of the leverage.

**Healthcare ransomware hit its highest quarterly total on record**, and the operational impact is now physical: a Canadian hospital's attack on its **facility-management system affected building doors and HVAC**, while US systems have had EHR platforms taken offline and care disrupted.

**The Gentlemen** — a RaaS operation offering affiliates up to **90% of proceeds** (versus the usual 70–80%), with 1,123 claimed victims across 95 countries — listed **NVIDIA** on its leak site on 18 November with a ~234-hour countdown. NVIDIA's position: it is investigating an incident that *may have affected a third-party partner*, and has *found no indication* its own systems or networks were affected. No sample files, screenshots or scope detail were published. **Treat this as an unsubstantiated claim, not a breach.**

On the enforcement side, **Ukraine and the FBI arrested four operators** tied to **$40.5M in paid ransoms** across at least 28 companies — a 27-person organisation with links to a 60+ member network including **former Conti affiliates**. Their tradecraft was methodical: research the victim's financial standing, assets and IT weaknesses first, then steal data and encrypt. One victim lost 1.4 TB and negotiated a $10M demand down to $5M in Bitcoin; **Chaos** and **BlackSuit** were the payloads.

> **ATT&CK:** T1486 Data Encrypted for Impact · T1657 Financial Theft · T1490 Inhibit System Recovery · T1567 Exfiltration Over Web Service · T1489 Service Stop · T1199 Trusted Relationship

## 3. Operational technology is now a direct attack surface, not a theoretical one

Attackers tampered with **electronic temperature controllers** in retail grocery and cold-storage facilities: fridge setpoints pushed to **50 °C**, freezers forced into defrost, food spoiled, and in some cases **fires started**. Hundreds of European supermarkets were hit across two waves, plus a **French blood bank** (quick staff action prevented loss of blood products).

CISA, the FBI, HHS, USDA and DoT issued a **joint advisory** naming Copeland/Dixell XWEB EVO and Danfoss StoreView controllers. Vulncheck disclosed 14 Copeland XWEB EVO vulnerabilities and saw two targeted almost immediately. Censys measured the response: internet-exposed controllers fell from **6,695 to 3,124** after the warning — an encouraging 53% reduction, but **47% remain exposed**.

Attribution is genuinely unresolved. Russian hacktivist groups **Noname057(16)** and **Z-Pentest Alliance** claimed a related intrusion into Danfoss, but Iranian hacktivists or state-sponsored actors are not excluded.

> **ATT&CK (ICS matrix):** T0836 Modify Parameter · T0831 Manipulation of Control · T0813 Denial of Control → T0826 Loss of Availability. In enterprise terms the same idea appears as **T1565.001 Stored Data Manipulation**.

## 4. Attackers are getting faster, and AI is part of the reason

Two independent observations this week, and they point the same direction:

- **Cloudflare** saw a single IP address used for both **Artex** (an open-source AI penetration-testing framework that threat actors abuse to automate intrusions) *and* Java4Shell activity — the first time the two were observed from one host. They also caught an actor probing with a `java4shell-recon` user agent, then returning ~17 hours later impersonating Chrome.
- **F6** found attacker scripts in the Cisco Expressway intrusion whose source comments and fix descriptions indicated **an AI assistant wrote and then patched them mid-operation**.

Combine that with public PoCs becoming working exploits in days, and the defensible position is **patch velocity plus behaviour-based detection**, not signature chasing.

> **ATT&CK:** T1588.002 Obtain Capabilities: Tool · T1587 Develop Capabilities · T1595 Active Scanning · T1036 Masquerading · T1059.006 Python

## 5. The authentication thread running through everything

A striking proportion of this week's serious items are, at bottom, **authentication failures** — and each one ends with an attacker using a service, integration or admin account:

- **SonicWall SonicOS SSLVPN CVE-2026-19868** — a user with valid credentials for an *alternate* auth method can bypass SAML controls, **and bypass SAML-enforced MFA**. You believe MFA is enforced; the product is not enforcing it on that path. KEV remediation was due **20 November** — i.e. now.
- **Barracuda ESG CVE-2026-116606 + CVE-2026-111560** — unauthenticated attacker restores a crafted backup archive to rewrite the appliance's global configuration, then chains an OS command injection to **root**. Affects seven Barracuda product lines. Mandiant-disclosed.
- **BerriAI LiteLLM CVE-2026-40217** — improper authentication in the LLM gateway, rated by FIRST at **62% probability of exploitation in the next 30 days**, the highest on this week's KEV list. An AI gateway is effectively a credential vault for every upstream model key.
- **Ivanti Connect Secure** — a three-CVE chain reaching **root without logging in**, then targeting **Active Directory credentials, LAPS passwords and BitLocker recovery keys**.
- **JFrog Artifactory CVE-2026-82329** — exploited within days of disclosure, with attackers **minting themselves admin tokens** in a supply-chain crown jewel.
- **Microsoft patched an Entra ID vulnerability that was already exploited** in the same week it patched 18 AI and cloud product flaws; **Splunk patched ~140** issues across three core products — including in the SIEM you rely on as your source of truth.

> **ATT&CK:** T1078 Valid Accounts · T1550 Use Alternate Authentication Material · T1548 Abuse Elevation Control Mechanism · T1098 Account Manipulation · T1555 Credentials from Password Stores · T1606 Forge Web Credentials

> ⚠️ **Read the ranking with one caveat.** The count is derived from ATT&CK's technique→mitigation mappings, and those mappings are incomplete for cross-cutting controls. **M1053 Data Backup scores only 5 events even though it is relevant to all four ransomware/extortion items** — ATT&CK simply doesn't map it from most techniques. Where the mechanical ranking and my judgement disagree, I say so below.

### My priority order, and where it differs

**1. M1051 Update Software (ranked 6th, but do this first).** Six separate exploited-in-the-wild products — vCenter, NGINX, PHP, Apache Axis/Confluence, Apache Roller, Cisco Expressway — all resolve to *"patch this week."* There is no clever compensating control for a CVSS 9.8 with a public PoC. It ranks 6th only because patching is mapped from a narrow set of techniques; in practice it is the single highest-yield action available to you.

**2. M1030 Network Segmentation + M1035 Limit Access (ranked 1st and 21st).** This is how you stop a compromised *appliance* — firewall, VPN concentrator, hypervisor, artifact repository, email gateway — from becoming a compromised *enterprise*. Management interfaces for vCenter, PAN-OS, SonicWall SSLVPN and Barracuda ESG should never be internet-facing. Critically, this is also the control that keeps a ransomware event from reaching **building-management systems**: IT and OT networks must not share a broadcast domain.

**3. M1032 MFA + M1015 identity hardening + M1026 Privileged Account Management (ranked 3rd, 18th, 2nd).** vCenter, Apache Axis and SonicWall are authentication failures at root, and every one of them ends with an attacker holding a **service or integration account**. Inventory those accounts, scope them to least privilege, require MFA, and — this is the SonicWall lesson — **verify MFA is actually enforced on every authentication path**, rather than assuming the vendor enforces it.

**4. M1047 Audit + M1057 Data Loss Prevention (ranked 10th and 13th by count, but 9 tactics each).** These are the only controls that reliably fire **before** extortion. Modern ransomware exfiltrates first: the Axis intrusion reached a cloud data warehouse in four hours, and the Conti-alumni crew walked off with 1.4 TB. Detect the terabytes leaving, the anomalous bulk CRM export, the new admin API token — not the encryption, which is already too late.

**5. M1053 Data Backup (ranked last of the quoted eight, but essential).** Immutable, offline, restoration-tested backups are what convert a $500M demand into an inconvenience. Note the Synology DSM KEV entry specifically: **attackers target the NAS precisely because it holds your backups.** Treat this as a top-three control for ransomware regardless of what the mechanical ranking says.

**6. M1040 Behaviour Prevention on Endpoint.** Covers AI-written tooling, novel RATs and SLIMSAVAGE-style web shells that signatures will miss.

**7. If you run OT: M0801 Restrict Network Access, then M0800 Least Privilege, M0804 Human User Authentication, M0810 Network Allowlists.** Taking a refrigeration controller off the internet *is* the fix — it is the agencies' first recommendation, and exposure dropped 53% within a month of the advisory, proving it is achievable.

**8. Standing controls: M1056 Pre-compromise and M1019 Threat Intelligence Program.** This week's attackers researched victim finances *before* making contact and iterated exploits *per product* within days. You want their playbook before they use it on you.

## 9. Caveats to carry into any decision

- **This is third-party reporting.** Verify against the linked primary source before acting — especially breach counts and attribution.
- **Severity reflects reported impact, not your exposure.** NGINX2Root only bites if HTTP/2 is enabled on a reachable listener; Confluence/Axis only if the legacy Axis surface is present. Researchers explicitly warned that exposure claims based purely on version numbers are misleading. Check configuration.
- **Attribution is provisional throughout.** Armory Castle is *suspected* China-nexus. The refrigeration attacks were *claimed* by Russian hacktivists but Iranian or state-sponsored actors are not excluded. The NSA NSANet and European Commission incidents have no public attribution at all — and an availability outage is not proof of an intrusion.
- **The NVIDIA item is not a confirmed breach.** It is an unsubstantiated leak-site listing with a countdown timer and no evidence. NVIDIA points to a possible third-party partner.
- **KEV dates are deadlines, not disclosure dates.** SonicWall's remediation date was 20 November; several others fall in the next 10 days.
